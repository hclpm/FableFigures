## Stem · Progenitors

| Asset | Visual description | Biological basis |
|---|---|---|
| [Pluripotent stem](./pluripotent.svg) | Round embryonic stem cell with a very large central nucleus and scant teal cytoplasm. | Pluripotent stem cells have a high nucleus-to-cytoplasm ratio, prominent nucleoli, and relatively sparse cytoplasmic specialization. |
| [Blood stem cell](./bloodstem.svg) | Round hematopoietic stem cell with a prominent nucleus, thin purple cytoplasm, and subtle membrane ruffles. | Hematopoietic stem cells are non-adherent suspension cells; their compact morphology and limited cytoplasm distinguish them from mature myeloid cells. |

| [B-cell precursor](./bprecursor.svg) | Round B-cell precursor with a large nucleus, thin green cytoplasm, and early B-lineage receptors. | B-cell precursors are small lymphoid cells with condensed chromatin and limited cytoplasm before maturation into receptor-rich B cells. |

## Lymphocytes · Immune

| Asset | Visual description | Biological basis |
|---|---|---|
| [NK cell](./nkcell.svg) | Round NK lymphocyte with an indented nucleus and a small set of dark cytotoxic granules. | NK cells are large granular lymphocytes; perforin- and granzyme-containing lysosomal granules polarize toward target-cell contacts. |
| [T cell](./tcell.svg) | Small round T lymphocyte with a large nucleus, narrow blue cytoplasm, and distributed TCR complexes. | Resting T cells are non-adherent lymphocytes with scant cytoplasm; T-cell receptors and CD3 complexes are distributed across the plasma membrane. |

| [Memory cell](./memory.svg) | Compact memory lymphocyte with large nucleus, narrow deep-blue cytoplasm, and a small activation-memory marker. | Quiescent memory lymphocytes resemble small resting lymphocytes but retain long-lived antigen-experienced receptor states. |
| [Immune cell](./immune.svg) | Generic leukocyte with irregular lobed nucleus, cytoplasmic granules, and flexible cell membrane. | Leukocytes use a deformable cortex for migration and lineage-dependent nuclear lobulation for movement through confined tissue spaces. |
| [Macrophage](./macrophage.svg) | Large amoeboid macrophage with pseudopods, bean-shaped nucleus, phagosomes, lysosomes, and engulfed cargo. | Macrophages extend actin-rich pseudopods around particles and route engulfed material through phagosomes to acidic lysosomes. |

## Gut epithelium

| Asset | Visual description | Biological basis |
|---|---|---|
| [Enterocyte](./enterocyte.svg) | Tall packed absorptive enterocyte with apical brush border, terminal web, lateral junctions, elongated basal nucleus, and mitochondria. | Enterocytes form a polarized columnar monolayer; dense apical microvilli increase absorptive surface while junctional complexes seal neighboring cells. |
| [Goblet cell](./goblet.svg) | Packed goblet cell with expanded apical mucin theca, narrow basal stalk, compressed basal nucleus, and secretion opening. | Goblet cells package heavily glycosylated mucins into large apical granules that expand the cell apex and discharge into the lumen. |
| [Paneth cell](./paneth.svg) | Pyramidal crypt-base Paneth cell with basal nucleus, rough ER, and numerous bright apical antimicrobial granules. | Paneth cells occupy the small-intestinal crypt base and secrete alpha-defensins, lysozyme, and other antimicrobials from apically concentrated dense-core granules. |
| [Endocrine cell](./endocrine.svg) | Narrow enteroendocrine epithelial cell with apical sensing process and basal dense-core secretory granules facing a capillary. | Enteroendocrine cells sense luminal cues and release peptide hormones basolaterally toward nerves and fenestrated capillaries. |
| [Tuft cell](./tuft.svg) | Narrow tuft cell with long stiff apical microvilli, actin rootlets, tubulovesicular network, and basal process. | Tuft cells are polarized chemosensory epithelial cells defined by unusually long actin-supported microvilli and an apical tubulovesicular system. |
| [Transit-amplifying](./transitamp.svg) | Packed crypt epithelial progenitor in mitosis with condensed chromosomes, spindle poles, and a beginning cleavage furrow. | Transit-amplifying cells divide rapidly above the crypt stem-cell zone before differentiating along absorptive or secretory lineages. |
| [Stem cell](./iecstem.svg) | Single slender crypt-base columnar stem cell with a narrow apical surface, gently tapered body, basal oval nucleus, and sparse cytoplasmic organelles. | LGR5-positive crypt-base columnar stem cells alternate with Paneth cells and continuously generate the intestinal epithelial lineages. |
| [M cell](./mcell.svg) | Follicle-associated epithelial M cell with flat sparse-microfold apex, captured antigen, transcytotic vesicles, and a basolateral lymphocyte pocket. | M cells sample luminal particles and deliver them across the epithelium to lymphocytes and mononuclear phagocytes housed in a basolateral pocket. |

## Molecular

| Asset | Visual description | Biological basis |
|---|---|---|
| [Cell](./cell.svg) | General eukaryotic cell with plasma membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, nucleolus, mitochondria, ER, and Golgi. | A eukaryotic cell is compartmentalized by membrane-bound organelles that separate genome maintenance, biosynthesis, trafficking, and energy production. |
| [Nucleus](./nucleus.svg) | Nucleus with double envelope, nuclear pores, peripheral chromatin, euchromatin, and a dense nucleolus. | The nuclear envelope is continuous with ER and perforated by pore complexes; nucleoli assemble ribosomal subunits within chromatin-containing nucleoplasm. |
| [Mitochondrion](./mitochondria.svg) | Cutaway mitochondrion with outer membrane, inner boundary membrane, folded cristae, matrix, and mitochondrial DNA. | Oxidative-phosphorylation complexes occupy the cristae-rich inner membrane, while the matrix contains metabolic enzymes, ribosomes, and mitochondrial DNA. |
| [ER](./er.svg) | Interconnected rough ER cisternae continuous with the nuclear envelope and studded by cytosolic ribosomes. | Rough ER ribosomes synthesize secreted and membrane proteins into flattened cisternae for folding and quality control. |
| [Golgi](./golgi.svg) | Polarized Golgi stack with cis and trans faces, flattened cisternae, and incoming and budding transport vesicles. | Golgi cisternae modify and sort biosynthetic cargo as it moves from the cis entry face toward the trans-Golgi network. |
| [Vesicle](./vesicle.svg) | Membrane-bound transport vesicle with lipid bilayer, luminal cargo, and a partial protein coat. | Transport vesicles bud with selective coats and cargo adaptors, then fuse with target membranes using tethering and SNARE machinery. |
| [DNA](./dna.svg) | Antiparallel double helix with sugar-phosphate backbones, paired bases, major groove, and minor groove. | DNA stores information in complementary base pairing while the helical geometry creates major and minor grooves recognized by proteins. |
| [RNA](./rna.svg) | Single-stranded RNA with ribose-phosphate backbone, exposed bases, stem-loop hairpin, and 5-prime/3-prime direction. | RNA is usually single-stranded but forms intramolecular base-paired stems and loops that support recognition and catalysis. |
| [Protein](./protein.svg) | Folded protein ribbon showing alpha helices, beta sheet, connecting loops, and a small ligand-binding pocket. | Protein tertiary structure packs secondary-structure elements into a three-dimensional fold whose surface pockets support molecular recognition. |
| [Antibody](./antibody.svg) | Y-shaped IgG with paired heavy chains, paired light chains, hinge, Fc stem, and two antigen-binding tips. | Immunoglobulin heavy and light variable domains form two antigen-binding sites, while the Fc region recruits immune effector mechanisms. |
| [Virus](./virus.svg) | Enveloped icosahedral virus with genome-containing capsid, lipid envelope, and surface attachment spikes. | Many animal viruses enclose a nucleocapsid within a host-derived lipid envelope bearing glycoprotein spikes used for receptor binding and entry. |
| [Bacterium](./bacterium.svg) | Rod-shaped bacillus with layered envelope, cytoplasm, nucleoid, ribosomes, pili, and flagellum. | Bacteria lack a membrane-bound nucleus; chromosomal DNA occupies a nucleoid, ribosomes fill the cytoplasm, and surface appendages mediate adhesion or motility. |
